3. Reading: What's It Like to Study Medicine?

Dr Fred Clough recounts his experience:
Medicine isn’t quite like other university degrees. I spent the first three years studying anatomy, cell biology, and pathology (what happens when the body goes wrong). I found that I was required to memorise far more than I had had to at secondary school and put in far longer hours, especially around exam periods.

In my final academic year, I studied for an extra degree in Physiological Sciences. I was also able to pursue areas of interest which in my case included neuroscience, and take supplementary modules in the history of medicine. Most universities now expect you to do some academic research as well. However, I felt incredibly lucky that I had the opportunity to work in prestigious laboratories where I rubbed shoulders with Nobel Prize winners.

As a clinical student, in other words as a student in a teaching hospital, my timetable changed dramatically. I was expected to dress smartly, as I spent every day with real patients as a functioning part of a medical team. These included surgery, dermatology, neurology, and psychiatry. I moved to a London hospital for my clinical training, which brought many memorable and emotionally taxing experiences.

4. Grammar: Relative Pronouns & Relative Clauses

Grammar Breakdown: Defining vs. Non-Defining Relative Clauses

1. Relative Pronouns:

• Who / That: for people (The doctor who / that treated me was very attentive).
• Which / That: for things/animals (The medication which / that was prescribed worked fast).
• Whose: for possession (A patient whose test results were delayed).
• Where / When: for places / times (The hospital where he trained).


2. Defining vs. Non-Defining:

• Defining (no commas): Essential information. That can replace who/which. The pronoun can be omitted if followed by subject + verb: The book (which) I read.
• Non-Defining (between commas): Extra non-essential information. NEVER use "that" and NEVER omit the pronoun: Dr Clough, who studied in London, is now a surgeon.
